# Project Nexus — 20-Query Presentation Outline

This is a presentation scaffold, not slide content.
Each query is a slide prompt that introduces the idea of the project in a logical sequence.

## Query 01
**Question:** What is Project Nexus in one sentence?
**Idea:** Open with the product identity: a graph-native investigation workspace for connected operational data.

## Query 02
**Question:** What kind of problem is this project built to solve?
**Idea:** Frame the core pain: important signals live across disconnected systems, so teams lose the relationships between people, assets, events, and decisions.

## Query 03
**Question:** Why do traditional tools struggle with this problem?
**Idea:** Contrast static tables, dashboards, and tickets with the need to understand chains of connection, not isolated records.

## Query 04
**Question:** What changes when the unit of analysis is a relationship instead of a row?
**Idea:** Introduce the graph mindset: value comes from seeing how entities connect, influence, and depend on one another.

## Query 05
**Question:** Who is this platform for?
**Idea:** Position the audience as investigation-driven teams such as revenue operations, risk, security, and analysts working across complex workflows.

## Query 06
**Question:** What are the core building blocks inside Nexus?
**Idea:** Present the conceptual model: entities, relationships, ontology, alerts, actions, views, and cases.

## Query 07
**Question:** Why is the ontology a first-class part of the system?
**Idea:** Explain that teams define their own domain model instead of hard-coding every business concept into the product.

## Query 08
**Question:** How does data enter the workspace?
**Idea:** Show the platform as an intake layer that can absorb structured information from CSV, JSON, and webhook flows.

## Query 09
**Question:** What happens to data after ingestion?
**Idea:** Emphasize that data is not just stored; it becomes searchable, connected, and ready for investigation.

## Query 10
**Question:** How does an analyst begin an investigation?
**Idea:** Introduce the operating flow: start from a search, an entity, or a saved view, then expand outward through the graph.

## Query 11
**Question:** What does the graph view actually help reveal?
**Idea:** Focus on patterns that are hard to see in lists: clusters, bottlenecks, hidden dependencies, and impact paths.

## Query 12
**Question:** Where does AI fit into the product?
**Idea:** Place AI as a signal-generation layer that adds alerts, hypotheses, recommendations, and review objects into the graph.

## Query 13
**Question:** How are AI signals kept operational instead of decorative?
**Idea:** Show that alerts can be reviewed, acknowledged, dismissed, or escalated into real investigation work.

## Query 14
**Question:** Why are cases part of the same system?
**Idea:** Connect insight to execution: a finding should become a trackable case without leaving the graph context.

## Query 15
**Question:** How does Nexus support team collaboration?
**Idea:** Present saved views, shared investigation state, linked entities, and action history as the collaboration layer.

## Query 16
**Question:** How does the project maintain trust and traceability?
**Idea:** Highlight provenance, review status, auditability, and the ability to see how a conclusion was reached.

## Query 17
**Question:** Why is this platform described as domain-agnostic?
**Idea:** Show that the same graph-and-ontology model can support multiple verticals without rebuilding the product each time.

## Query 18
**Question:** What is the clearest demo scenario for this version of the project?
**Idea:** Anchor the story in the current revenue-operations example where accounts, contracts, invoices, tickets, and alerts form an investigation graph.

## Query 19
**Question:** What makes Nexus an operational workspace rather than just a visualization tool?
**Idea:** Surface the system features around administration, tenant isolation, ingestion controls, health visibility, and workflow state.

## Query 20
**Question:** What is the bigger idea behind Project Nexus?
**Idea:** Close on the thesis: give smaller teams a practical way to model complex reality, investigate faster, and act from connected context.
